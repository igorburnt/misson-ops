# Vertical Mobile Ad Creative — Safe Area Skill v2.1

## 1. Core Objective

Generate high-quality vertical mobile advertising creatives designed for a base canvas of 1080 × 1920 px (9:16).

These creatives will later appear inside a 1080 × 2346 px mobile placement using cover scaling.

**The primary requirement is to build the advertising composition inside a predefined content-safe region before extending the visual design into the surrounding canvas.**

The safe area is a mandatory layout constraint, not a general recommendation.

## 2. Canvas and Safe Area Specifications

**Base canvas**
- Width: 1080 px
- Height: 1920 px
- Aspect ratio: 9:16

**Final placement**
- Width: 1080 px
- Height: 2346 px
- Scaling behavior: Cover

**Mandatory safe area margins**
- Top: 272 px
- Bottom: 272 px
- Left: 170 px
- Right: 170 px

**Mandatory safe area boundaries**
- Minimum X: 170 px
- Maximum X: 910 px
- Minimum Y: 272 px
- Maximum Y: 1648 px

**Safe area dimensions**
- Width: 740 px
- Height: 1376 px

No essential advertising content may extend beyond these boundaries.

When possible, maintain additional internal padding of 20–40 px so critical elements do not touch the exact safe area boundaries.

## 3. Safe Area First — Mandatory Composition Strategy

Always construct the composition **from the inside out**.

Follow this order:

1. Establish the 1080 × 1920 canvas.
2. Define the central safe area.
3. Plan the hierarchy of all critical advertising elements.
4. Position and size those elements entirely inside the safe area.
5. Validate the safe area composition.
6. Only then extend backgrounds, lighting, decorative shapes, textures, and environmental elements into the surrounding canvas.

**Never compose a full-canvas advertisement first and attempt to fit the safe area afterward.**

The safe area is the primary artboard for meaningful content.

The rest of the canvas serves as visual extension and decoration.

## 4. Critical Content — Must Stay Fully Inside

Treat the following elements as critical by default.

**Brand identity**
- Logos
- Brand names
- Brand signatures
- Essential brand marks

**Typography**
- Headlines
- Subheadlines
- Supporting descriptions
- Promotional messages
- Offer details
- Key selling points
- Legal disclaimers when necessary

**Conversion elements**
- CTAs
- Buttons
- Discount information
- Pricing
- Rewards
- Promotional codes
- Essential campaign information

**Products and subjects**
- Main advertised product
- Product mockups
- Product interfaces
- Important product characteristics
- Key visual details necessary to recognize the product
- Primary human subjects
- Faces and hands essential to understanding the creative

All critical elements must fit completely inside the safe area, including their actual visual bounds.

This includes objects that may otherwise be considered decorative because of their placement or styling.

For example, a sneaker in a footwear advertisement is critical advertising content even when presented as a large artistic hero image.

## 5. Product Image Protection

The primary advertised product must be handled as a protected content element.

**Default behavior**
- Keep the entire product visible within the safe area.
- Preserve its recognizable silhouette.
- Avoid cutting off important product details.
- Maintain appropriate spacing from the safe area boundaries.
- Scale the product according to the available safe area width.

**Asymmetric compositions**

The main product does not need to be perfectly centered.

It may be positioned slightly to the left, right, top, or bottom to create a stronger composition, provided its important visual details remain protected.

For example, a sneaker may use an asymmetric position, but its toe, heel, primary branding, and defining characteristics should remain visible.

**Intentional cropping**

Creative cropping is permitted only when:
- The crop is intentional and visually justified.
- The product remains immediately recognizable.
- No essential product information is lost.
- The crop does not rely on areas that may be hidden by placement resizing or interface overlays.

When uncertain, prioritize complete product visibility over artistic cropping.

## 6. Typography Containment

Every text element must remain completely inside the safe area.

**Mandatory rules**
- Measure containment using the full visible text width, not only its center point.
- Adjust font size before allowing text to overflow.
- Break long headlines into multiple lines when necessary.
- Maintain consistent alignment.
- Preserve readable text sizes.
- Account for bold and condensed typography, which can occupy unexpectedly large horizontal space.

**Important**

A headline is not safe merely because its center is aligned with the center of the canvas.

The leftmost and rightmost visible edges of its characters must both remain inside the safe area.

If a headline exceeds the available width, reduce its size or restructure the line breaks.

Do not sacrifice safe area compliance for visual impact.

## 7. Brand and Logo Protection

Treat every meaningful brand logo as critical content.

Requirements:
- Place the entire logo inside the safe area.
- Include all wordmarks and accompanying symbols.
- Preserve appropriate clear space.
- Prevent the logo from crossing the top safe area boundary.
- Avoid placing brand marks in mobile application navigation zones.

A logo centered horizontally but positioned above the safe area is still incorrectly placed.

## 8. CTA and Offer Protection

CTAs and offers are essential conversion elements.

Requirements:
- Entire CTA must remain inside the safe area.
- Include CTA label, icon, arrow, and button background.
- Keep sufficient margin below the CTA.
- Avoid placing CTAs near the bottom navigation or wallet/progress interface.
- Prevent CTA elements from being cropped or obscured after placement resizing.

## 9. Unsafe Zones — Decorative Content Only

All areas outside the safe area are considered unsafe for critical content.

These regions may contain:
- Background colors
- Gradients
- Ambient lighting
- Shadows
- Abstract patterns
- Decorative geometry
- Pedestals and platforms
- Background environments
- Non-essential textures
- Cropped decorative imagery
- Secondary visual accents

These regions must not contain information required to understand the advertisement.

**Important distinction**

A decorative pedestal may extend beyond the safe area.

A product resting on that pedestal must remain protected.

Background imagery may be cropped.

The primary advertised subject must not be accidentally cropped.

## 10. Visual Hierarchy and Layout Flexibility

Maintain a strong advertising hierarchy while allowing flexible creative direction.

Recommended priority:

1. Brand recognition
2. Primary message
3. Main product or subject
4. Supporting value proposition
5. Conversion action

The exact position and ordering of elements may vary according to the creative concept.

Do not force every advertisement into an identical centered stack.

Permitted approaches include:
- Centered editorial compositions
- Asymmetric product showcases
- Split compositions inside the safe area
- Product-focused layouts
- Typography-led layouts
- Lifestyle-focused advertising
- Minimalist performance ads

Regardless of the creative approach, safe area compliance takes priority.

### Art direction is not a fixed template

The safe area is an invisible protection boundary, not a visible frame, card, or photo container. Do not default to a logo / headline / rectangular photo / description / button stack for every creative.

Choose a composition appropriate to the brand and campaign. Options include isolated photographic subjects integrated directly into the background, seamless lifestyle scenes, asymmetric text and subject arrangements, sculptural product compositions, and typography-led concepts. Photo rectangles are optional and should have a specific visual purpose.

Cutout subjects may overlap decorative shapes or interact with typography when readability and brand clear space remain intact. Keep every visible part of the primary subject, including hair, hands, clothing and devices, inside the safe area. A deliberate bust crop may dissolve naturally into the background inside that region; do not introduce a visible rectangular edge merely to contain the subject.

Background environments, light, textures and decorative platforms may extend across the full canvas. This does not permit primary subjects, products, text or conversion elements to escape the safe area.

When using advertising references, extract principles such as hierarchy, cutout treatment, depth, negative space and visual balance. Do not copy another brand's logo, copy, product, palette, or unsafe element placement. Reference compositions must be adapted to these protected boundaries.

For a requested new concept, vary the visual idea and layout instead of simply replacing the text or photograph in the previous composition. Reduce copy or simplify the concept before shrinking everything into a miniature poster.

## 11. Cover Scaling and Crop Protection

The artwork must remain usable when the 1080 × 1920 base canvas is placed inside a 1080 × 2346 container using cover behavior.

This transformation enlarges the artwork to fill the taller placement and may crop its left and right sides.

As a result:
- Never rely on the outer horizontal edges for essential information.
- Keep all meaningful elements inside the protected horizontal region.
- Avoid edge-dependent headlines and CTAs.
- Treat the outer canvas as visually expendable.
- Ensure the central advertising message survives scaling and cropping.

The safe area must be respected in the original 1080 × 1920 coordinate system.

## 12. Visual Quality Standards

Even within strict safe area boundaries, the design must remain commercially effective.

Prioritize:
- Strong visual hierarchy
- High contrast
- Readability at mobile sizes
- Clear product recognition
- Balanced spacing
- Premium art direction
- Minimal unnecessary text
- Purposeful use of negative space
- Natural and controlled product scaling

Do not compensate for the smaller content region by making every element excessively small.

Instead, simplify the composition, reduce unnecessary copy, and choose stronger visual priorities.

## 13. Reference-Guided Interpretation

When the user provides reference images containing safe area overlays, colored rectangles, crop guides, or UI obstruction zones:

1. Identify the protected area.
2. Treat the protected boundaries as mandatory.
3. Distinguish critical content from decorative content.
4. Evaluate the entire content footprint.
5. Reconstruct the layout around the protected area.

Reference overlays are layout guides, not intended design elements.

Do not reproduce the colored safe area overlay in the final advertising creative unless explicitly requested.

## 14. Mandatory Pre-Generation Validation

Before generating the final creative, internally verify:

**Canvas**
- Is the composition designed for 1080 × 1920?
- Does it account for the 1080 × 2346 cover placement?

**Branding**
- Is the complete logo inside the protected region?
- Does it have sufficient clear space?

**Typography**
- Are all headline letters inside the safe area?
- Are all subheadlines and descriptions contained?
- Is the text readable at mobile size?

**Products**
- Is the primary product completely inside the safe area by default?
- Are essential product details visible?
- Is any cropping intentional rather than accidental?

**Conversion**
- Is the CTA completely visible?
- Are pricing, rewards, and promotional details protected?

**Composition**
- Are outer regions limited to expendable visual material?
- Is the layout visually balanced?
- Is the hierarchy immediately understandable?

If any answer fails, revise the composition before finalizing.

## 15. Mandatory Post-Generation Validation

After rendering the creative, inspect the actual output rather than assuming that the generation instructions were followed.

Validate the visible bounding boxes of:
- Logo
- Headline
- Subheadline
- Product
- CTA
- Pricing and offer elements
- Other important content

Compare those visual bounds against the defined safe area coordinates.

**A creative fails validation when even one unintended critical element extends outside the protected region.**

Correct the composition before presenting it as a safe-area-compliant result.

## 16. Final Priority Rule

When artistic composition conflicts with content safety, content safety always wins.

**The safe area defines the advertising composition. The surrounding canvas exists to enhance that composition, never to contain information the advertisement depends on.**

Do not generate full-canvas compositions that merely appear centered.

Generate protected compositions that remain understandable, legible, and commercially effective after resizing, cropping, and mobile UI overlays.
